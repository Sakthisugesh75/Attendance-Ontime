Attendance Desk: three directions

Same screens, three different ideas. Pick one and I'll build it into the app.

Time Card

The mechanical punch clock, made digital. Every punch is stamped into an IN or OUT slot on a card, and late punches print in red, just like the old time clock did. Staff already know how to read a time card, so nothing needs explaining.

Signature: the stamp. Tap "Punch in" and the time lands in today's slot with a small stamp impression.
Type: Archivo (condensed for headings, like a printed card) with Martian Mono only for stamped times.
Feels: sturdy, familiar, a little industrial. Good for factories, shops and offices that used punch cards.

Daylight

A workday is a stretch of daylight. Every screen draws the day as a line from morning to evening: your shift, the hours you've worked, and a marigold sun showing now. In the register each person is a bar on the same line, so HR spots who came late at a glance, without reading a single time.

Signature: the day line. It appears on the check-in screen, in the register and in the monthly grid (as filled hour bars).
Type: Schibsted Grotesk: bold, warm, very readable numbers.
Feels: bright, modern, optimistic. The most "app-like" of the three.

Muster Book

The paper attendance register every Indian office keeps, with ruled lines, a red margin and P / A marks, rebuilt for the phone. Employees sign today's line instead of "punching". The typeface was designed for low-vision readers, and the text is larger everywhere. This is the easiest of the three for staff who rarely use apps.

Signature: the ruled register page with its red margin. Signing writes your time onto today's line.
Type: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (made for legibility) with its Mono for times. 16px minimum.
Feels: official, calm, trustworthy. Closest to how HR already works on paper.
